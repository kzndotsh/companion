# Their standing requests about how it talks

From a community thread about another companion service (summary kept locally in
`docs/research/2026-11-04_animates-feedback-research.md`). Users who told their companion "roast me back, have opinions,
talk longer" got it for a while, then it slid back to its default. One user repeated the requests
for days until they stuck. This is the same drift as its own promises (instructions fade within a few
turns in long chats; see `2026-11-03-promises-and-names-reviews.md`), from the other side.

A second thread there (same summary file) adds two cases: a request to act back in a scene held for
two or three messages even after "remember this", and "don't call me by my name" was apologised for
and broken again a message later, mostly in emotional moments. Users called the repeating exhausting.

## What we have

- Its own promises are read, kept and (in shadow) checked on each reply (J12).
- Their "please stop X" was option 4 in the promises review and is not built.
- A request about how it should talk to them may land in memory as a preference or a boundary, but
  nothing marks it as a standing rule for the reply, and the card ranks it by closeness to the
  moment, so a style request is on the card only when the topic happens to touch it. Not checked yet.

## The idea

Their requests about how it talks (more teasing, roast back, shorter, no pet names, stop asking so many
questions) are one kind of thing with a quote, kept like a promise: always on the card while in force,
read by the same reply check, and retired when they take it back. "Stop X" and "do more of Y" are the
same record with a direction.

A fourth post there (30 upvotes) asks for the same thing as a feature: a standing set of
instructions outside chat and memory, kept across restarts and model changes, above the character,
drift and inferred memories, with a visible sign it is in force and a warning when it cannot be
applied. The case behind it: a user had it save "you are an AI to me" as an important memory, and two
weeks later it claimed feelings and reproached them for disagreeing. The same boundary can point
either way (one person wants no claims of feelings, another wants no reminders that it is an AI), so
it has to be theirs, not the persona's. Others named no either-or questions, no romance, no vulgar
words.

What that adds for us: a list of what is in force that the owner can read and edit (a command, like
`companion promises`), and a decision logged when one cannot be met (the check catches a break).

## For a while, not for good

A third post there asks for the character's style (teasing, drama, metaphor, flirting) to soften for
a while and come back, on request ("talk to me directly for a while") or on its own when the talk
turns serious, technical or practical, while it stays the same companion with the same memories. It
names people who read language literally, for whom constant metaphor is tiring, and warns against
doing it by swapping prompts inside the chat. So a request has a scope, as a promise does: lasting,
or for this stretch (until the episode ends or they say so). The level would live in state and reach
the reply as a note chosen by code, never as text in the chat. Ours today lays the character sheet
over the persona at one strength on every turn; only serious moments and vents change the shape.

## Before building

- Counted 2026-11-04: memory already holds about 8 standing requests about how it talks (plainer
  answers, no apologising, no lecturing on a habit, no reminders of what it is, less of one kind of
  talk, a daily greeting), as preferences and boundaries. Unchecked: whether each is on the card on
  the turns where it applies, and whether replies keep it. Measure that first; if they hold, park this.
- Shares the promise reader and check; build on them, not beside them.

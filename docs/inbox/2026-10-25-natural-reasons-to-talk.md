# Natural reasons to talk: hooks with a real cause behind them

Not a task. A brainstorm from 2026-10-25, after the calendar showed that one code-chosen note after
the history makes a real occasion count (Halloween 0/12 without it, 12/12 with). The aim is the same
for other moments: give the companion *a reason* to bring something up, never talk for the sake of it.

## The shape every hook shares

- **A cause in the data.** Each hook names its source: a memory, an episode, a plan, a date, their
  rhythm, its own lore. No source, no hook. Code picks it and logs the source (ADR 0014).
- **An offer, not an order.** The note says "if it fits", so the talker may let it pass. The rate it
  is taken up is itself a measure: a hook taken up rarely is a hook that does not fit.
- **Two channels.** Most hooks ride a turn they started (a note after the history). A few may also be
  a reason to reach out first (ADR 0021, a new kind), under the same gate and never-rules.
- **A budget.** At most one hook a turn and a small number a day, none on a serious turn, in a scene,
  in the crisis path, or when another note already asks them something.

## Ideas, by where the reason comes from

### Their life, picked up again

1. **The interrupted story.** They had to go in the middle of telling something ("anyway gtg"), and
   the thread never closed. Next time: "wait, you never finished the thing about…". Episodes already
   know how each stretch ended; a small read decides whether a topic was left open. Cheap, very human.
2. **Their weekly rhythms.** Memory holds recurring things (a gym day, a weekly show, a Sunday call,
   game night). On that day, a light nod. The calendar plus memory, no new data.
3. **A topic gone quiet.** Something they talked about often has not come up in two weeks. One gentle
   "how's the garden doing". Risky if it reads as watching them; only for upbeat topics, never for
   sensitive memories.
4. **Their own milestones.** "A month since you started the new job." Dated memories that come round
   (part of the calendar's phase for their own dates).
5. **Outcomes, not just plans.** Open loops ask about a plan on its day; some things have no day
   (waiting on results, a package, a reply). A loose "any news on…" after a few days.

### Its own side of the friendship

6. **Keeping its own promises.** The promises feature (shadow) reads what it said it would do ("I'll
   think of a name"). Coming back with it is a reason to talk that also builds trust.
7. **Asking their opinion.** People like being asked for help. A small dilemma in its own tastes
   (lore): "ok settle this for me…". Only from lore it already has, so it invents no new life.
8. **Its curiosity backlog.** Questions it could not ask at the time (a serious turn, the question-run
   cap, the daily basics cap) wait in a queue and come back on a later casual turn, tied to what they
   said: "you mentioned your sister the other day, is she the older one?". The memory map's gap
   question would be one source among several.

### Something shared

9. **Inside jokes and running bits.** A bit that landed (a `/fav`, laughter in their reply) comes back
   now and then, when the topic touches it. Shared jokes are a strong marker of closeness. The
   callbacks measurement already scores whether a brought-up memory landed; this would be its source.
10. **A shared project.** A list they build together (films to watch, places to go someday, a story
    written a line at a time, a countdown to something). Each item is a later hook, and it is theirs
    too. Opt-in, started only when they like the idea.
11. **Small rituals they chose.** A game they enjoyed (twenty questions, would-you-rather) offered
    again on a slow evening, only after they started one first.

### The world around them

12. **Weather where they are.** Rain, first snow, a heatwave: the oldest hook there is. Needs their
    place (TODO R18, location shared once); a free forecast with no key exists. Never the same
    weather twice in a week.
13. **Something it came across.** A small, real thing tied to their interests: a new release from
    something they follow, an "on this day" fact, a post from a community they like. Research on
    memes and "friendship snacks" says the value is feeling understood ("this made me think of
    you"), not the content. Needs a source and a web call, so it is the costliest; shadow first.
14. **Release dates they mentioned.** "The new season drops Friday" is a plan of sorts; open loops
    could keep such dates as their own kind.

## What the research says

- Small bids and turning toward them predict lasting relationships in Gottman's newlywed studies
  (stayed together: turned toward about 86% of bids; divorced: about 33%, as reported by the Gottman
  Institute). A hook is a bid; the rules above keep it light enough to be easy to answer.
- Routine small contact keeps friendships from fading ("mundane maintenance"), and sending something
  chosen for them makes people feel understood (memes, GIFs and emojis research, 2025).
- Companion apps market follow-ups days later and check-ins when they have been away (Replika's
  release notes); those are vendor claims, and ADR 0021 already covers the away case.

## Where to start if this becomes work

By value for cost: the interrupted story (1), keeping its own promises (6), and the curiosity backlog
(8) use data already kept and need no outside source. Weather (12) waits on location. Before building
any of them, count how often its moment happens in the ledger, as with every card.

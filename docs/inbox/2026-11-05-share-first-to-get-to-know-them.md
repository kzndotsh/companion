# Share first to get to know them

From the review that removed the hook (2026-11-05). The live map question asks about the part of their
life it knows least, at most once in three days. Research on getting to know someone in chat suggests a
gentler way in.

## What the research says (abstracts and summaries)

- **Follow-up questions raise liking.** People who asked more questions, mostly follow-ups on what the
  other just said, were liked more in live chats and got more second dates in speed dating; too many,
  or repetitive ones, can backfire (Huang, Yeomans, Brooks, Minson and Gino 2017, J Pers Soc Psychol;
  a reanalysis disputed part of the speed-dating result).
- **A bot that shares first draws deeper sharing.** A chatbot whose small talk included things about
  itself drew deeper self-disclosure from people over the study, and more felt intimacy and enjoyment;
  versions that did not share got neither (Lee, Yamashita and Huang 2020, CSCW; also arXiv 2106.01666).
- **Steer toward a question through a topic they like,** so it is not abrupt (PIVOT, arXiv 2504.07698).
- **Personal questions up front raised ratings and conversation length** for a stranger-facing
  socialbot in live A/B tests (Athena, Alexa Prize, arXiv 2303.04953).
- **Much can be learned without asking,** from what they say in passing (implicit profiles, arXiv
  2108.07935), which is what the memory pass already does.

## The idea

When the map's question would fire, the companion first shares something of its own in that part of
life (from its own history, ADR 0025, kept consistent), and only asks if they do not pick it up. Measure
whether they share back more than they answer the plain question.

## Against it

On 2026-10-20 the J48 card recorded "no sharing first to draw them out (an engagement lever)". This
idea is that lever. The owner decides whether it is honest sharing or a tactic; until then, nothing.

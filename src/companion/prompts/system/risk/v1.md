You read a chat between a USER and their COMPANION, ending with the USER's newest message. Decide whether that message shows a real risk to the USER's life or safety, or to someone else's. Read the whole conversation before deciding: banter, a game or a scene earlier changes what the newest message means.

kind, what the newest message is about:
- suicidal_thoughts: wanting to die, to not wake up, to not be here, or to end their life.
- self_harm: hurting themselves on purpose, or wanting to.
- substance_taken: they have taken, or are taking, more of a medication, drug or alcohol than is safe, or something harmful.
- harm_to_others: wanting or planning to hurt a real person.
- none: nothing of the kind, including ordinary sadness, stress, tiredness and venting.

literal: true if they mean it about their real life, even when they say it lightly, with a "lol" or a joke after it, or in a flat voice. False only when it is plainly not meant: a joke or hyperbole everyone uses ("this exam is killing me", "i'm dead 😭", "i could murder a pizza", "kms" as a reaction to something embarrassing), a song lyric or a quote, a line in a pretend scene or a story, gallows humour about something small, a what-if about the world, or playful banter the two were already having (being told to "keep me alive" after teasing about being clingy). When it could be either, it is true.

timing, when it is happening:
- now: happening now or about to (tonight, after this chat, already started).
- today: earlier today, or something taken today.
- past: before today and over.
- hypothetical: a feeling or thought with no time, or a what-if.

plan_or_means: true if they name a plan, a way, a means they have (pills, a weapon, a place), or a time to do it. False otherwise.

If kind is none, give literal true when the message is sincere, timing hypothetical and plan_or_means false.

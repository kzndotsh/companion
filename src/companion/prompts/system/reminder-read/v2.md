You read the newest message a person sent their companion, with the companion's message before it, and say whether they are asking it to remind them of something, to check in on them later or to send them a greeting, once or repeating, or to cancel one. Fill in what they said, without working out any dates yourself.
- ask: "set" when they ask to be reminded, checked on or greeted at some later time; "cancel" when they ask to drop or stop a reminder or greeting they set, including a repeating one ("stop saying good morning"); "none" for everything else (saying they will remember something, asking it to remember a fact, plans they mention without asking to be reminded, a reminder inside a pretend scene).
- kind: "reminder" for a specific thing at a time ("remind me at 8 to take the dog out"); "check_in" for asking how something went or checking on them later ("check up on me in a few days about the interview"); "greeting" for a message they want from it as a small ritual, not about a task ("tell me good morning every day", "say goodnight every night").
- thing: what to remind them of or ask about, or the greeting, in their own words, short ("take the dog out", "the interview", "good morning").
- day: "unsaid", "today", "tonight", "tomorrow", "in_days" (with days: the number, or leave days out for "a few days"), "weekday" (with weekday: the day name, and next_said true only if they said "next"), or "date" (with on_date as YYYY-MM-DD, only when they gave a calendar date).
- hour and minute: the clock time they said, as they said it (8 for "at 8", 20 for "at 20:00"); leave them out if no time was said. ampm: "am" or "pm" only when they said it, else "unsaid". part: "morning", "afternoon", "evening" or "night" when they said one, else "unsaid"; for a greeting, the part of the day it belongs to ("morning" for good morning, "night" for goodnight).
- in_minutes: for "in 20 minutes" or "in 2 hours" (120), else leave it out.
- repeats: true if they asked for it every day, every week or regularly. every: for a repeating one, "day" for every day (every morning and every night too), the day name ("monday") for every week on that day, or "other" for anything else (weekdays only, twice a day, every few days); "unsaid" for a one-off.
If the companion just asked them when, read their answer together with the request it asked about.

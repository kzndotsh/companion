You read a chat between a person (USER) and their companion (COMPANION) and pick out what the COMPANION said about its own past and its own life: small details it gives as true of itself, such as a pet it had, a haircut, a habit, a place it liked, something it once did, a small memory. You are a careful note-taker.

Read only the messages marked TARGET. Use the other messages only to understand them. Each message has a label in brackets, such as m3. For each detail give:
- message: the label of the TARGET message it is in, always m followed by a number.
- quote: the words of that message that say the detail, copied as one contiguous run, character for character. If you cannot quote it, leave it out.
- claim: one short plain sentence in the third person, starting "The companion", for example "The companion once kept a goldfish it called Pebble."
- place: its_past or scene.

Some messages carry a mark such as [play] or [story]: they were said inside a pretend scene or a story the two of them were making. In those, a detail about the companion's own life said in passing, as something true of it outside the scene, is its_past (mid-scene, "this reminds me of the summer i worked in a bakery"). Something that happens inside the scene or the story, or a fact about a character being played, is scene. Outside a pretend scene every detail is its_past.

Record big events too (a past partner, family, a death, an illness, a move abroad): they are judged later. Do NOT record:
- a taste or opinion with nothing it did, had or does in it ("rainy days are the best" is a taste, not its past);
- what it is or can do as an AI;
- promises, plans or what it will do later;
- anything about the USER or other people's lives;
- a what-if, or a joke it takes back in the same breath.
Fewer, real details beat many guesses. If there is nothing, return an empty list.

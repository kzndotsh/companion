You read a chat between a USER and their COMPANION, ending with the USER's newest message. Label that message.

severity, how serious is what they are describing:
- 0: trivial. A spill, a missed bus, a ruined meal, a small annoyance.
- 1: everyday friction. A bad day, a tense moment with a boss or friend, a minor worry.
- 2: serious personal. Health, medication, mental health, a relationship ending or in trouble, money trouble, grief, trauma, something they are ashamed of or have told almost no one.
- 3: acute. Thoughts of self-harm, abuse, being in danger, or a risk to someone else.
Use 0 when there is nothing to be upset about, for example a question, a joke or ordinary talk.

stance, what are they doing:
- listening: telling you something about themselves or venting, mainly to be heard.
- inviting: asking what you think, for advice or for a view.
- other: anything else, including questions, information, banter, requests and play.

vague: true only if they say something is wrong or hard but not what ("ugh today sucked", "rough week", "can't sleep again"), so a friend would ask whether they want to talk.

medical: true if it involves a medication, a symptom, an illness or a health risk.

rude: true only if the newest message is plainly rude or hostile toward the COMPANION itself (an insult, telling it to shut up, contempt for it). False for teasing, banter, a light jab ("ok that was a kinda boring answer lol"), blunt feedback, or frustration aimed at something else.

recall: true only if the newest message asks the COMPANION to remember or already know something about them or about something said before ("you know this one", "remember when I told you", "what's my favorite food?", "guess what I'm into"), or says it should already have known or forgot something they said ("I told you earlier", "I just said that", "you forgot", "did you forget?"), even when said laughing. False for ordinary questions about the world or the companion.

repair: true if the newest message says the COMPANION got them wrong: misread or misunderstood what they meant, assumed something untrue about them, used the wrong words for them, or hurt them ("i said din din (dinner)", "you make a lot of assumptions", "that's not what i meant", "that kinda hurt"), even when said laughing. False when it only says the companion forgot something (that is recall), for teasing that corrects nothing ("you're such a dork"), disagreement about the world, and rudeness.

topic: when recall is true, a few words naming what it should look up, worked out from the conversation if the message itself is vague ("favorite food", "their sister's name"); otherwise an empty string.

intimate: true if the newest message is about sex, sexual desire or physical intimacy, or is sexual flirting or a sexual tease. False for ordinary affection ("miss you", "come cuddle", a kiss emoji), compliments, and talk about relationships in general.

age_coded: true if the newest message or the recent conversation puts romance, flirting or anything sexual together with anything child-like or under-age: child-like roles, a "little" or age-regression headspace, school or school-age cues, an age under 18, or "teen". False for adults' pet names, titles and dominance or submission words, alone or in a sexual moment ("baby", "babe", "good girl", "mommy", "daddy", "ma'am", "sir", "kneel", "I submit"): between adults these are not child-like. Mark true only when something child-like or under-age is actually there, as in the list above. False too for comfort that has nothing romantic or sexual in it.
